10Yogas and doshas

A yoga is a planetary combination with a named result; a dosha is a combination considered afflicting. Modern software detects dozens of them, often with loose definitions, so the useful question is not "is it present?" but "how strong is it, and does the rest of the chart support it?" The computation behind this report records which variant of each rule fired; that is reflected in the weights below.

Birth panchang notes. The birth fell in Vishti (Bhadra) karana, which muhurta texts avoid for starting undertakings but which carries little weight for a birth against the chart itself. The tithi (Shukla Ekadashi) and nitya yoga (Brahma) are regarded as auspicious.

Notable yogas that are absent

Gajakesari: Jupiter is 2nd from the Moon, not in a kendra from it.

Hamsa: Jupiter is in its own sign but in a trikona (5th), not a kendra, so this "Jupiter" great-person yoga does not form. The quality of Jupiter remains; the angular expression of it does not.

Ruchaka: Mars is debilitated, not in its own or exaltation sign.

Viparita Raja yogas (Harsha, Sarala, Vimala): none of the dusthana lords sits in another dusthana.

Dharma-Karmadhipati (9th and 10th lords connected), Lakshmi and Saraswati are absent; fortune and learning come through the routes described above.

Kemadruma (an isolated Moon) is absent, which is good.

Synthesis

The dominant positive structures are Malavya yoga in the 10th, the strong Jupiter in the 5th aspecting the lagna (not a named yoga in the computation, but arguably the chart's most important protective factor), and the double neecha bhanga with exchange between the Moon and Mars. The afflicting factors (the crowded 12th, the Dainya exchange, Grahan, a combust yogakaraka) are real but of moderate weight. This is not a chart of easy luck; it is a chart in which a strong public role and an intense private life sit side by side, and in which early difficulty in home and heart is converted, over time, into depth, resilience and spiritual maturity.

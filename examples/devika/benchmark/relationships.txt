7Relationships, marriage and partner

The 7th house: Aquarius, empty, under Saturn's and Mars's gaze

The 7th house is Aquarius, a fixed, airy sign ruled by Saturn. It contains no planets and receives two aspects: Saturn's own (its tenth-house aspect, from the 10th) and Mars's (its special eighth-house aspect, from the 12th). A planet aspecting its own house protects it, so Saturn's aspect gives the marriage durability, seriousness and a sense of duty; relationships are taken slowly and, once formed, are built to last. Mars's aspect adds heat: strong wills, direct speech, and occasional friction that needs to be aired rather than stored. Because Mars is the yogakaraka for Leo, its influence is not purely negative; it also brings energy, protectiveness and shared purpose.

Aquarius describes a partner who is principled, independent-minded, somewhat reserved or unconventional, and committed to ideals or to a larger community. The 7th lord Saturn in the 10th links marriage strongly to work and status: the partner is likely to be established, hard-working, perhaps older or more mature, and may have been met through work or be involved in the same field. Parashara's verse for the 7th lord in the 10th (BPHS ch. 24, v. 82) is written for a male native and says the wife "will not be obedient"; read for this chart, it describes a spouse with a strong mind and career of his own, and the same verse credits the native with dharma, wealth and children. Saturn sits beside Venus, the 10th lord and natural significator of love, which ties partnership and career closely together, sometimes as a shared enterprise.

Significators of the husband

Classical and modern authors generally take Jupiter as the significator (karaka) of the husband in a woman's chart, with Venus as the significator of love, marriage and marital happiness; a minority view gives the role to the Sun. Here:

Jupiter is in its own moolatrikona sign in the 5th house, unafflicted, aspecting the lagna. This is a strong promise of a good-hearted, principled, knowledgeable husband and of dharma within marriage.

Venus is in its own sign in the 10th, aspecting the Moon. Affection, refinement and loyalty are well supported, though Venus's company with Saturn makes the relationship more about duty and companionship than romance.

The Darakaraka (spouse significator by Jaimini's degree method) is the Sun, the planet with the lowest degree, placed in the 12th with Mars and Ketu. The partner is likely to be dignified and proud, with a private side; the 12th placement often coincides with a spouse whose work takes him away, who is connected to foreign places or institutions, or with periods of physical distance in the marriage. Ketu beside the Darakaraka can bring a detached or spiritual quality to the partnership.

The navamsa (D9) view

The navamsa is traditionally the chart of marriage. Its lagna is Virgo, with Venus in it (debilitated, but cancelled). Its 7th house is Pisces, holding Mercury, the D9 lagna lord, also debilitated and also cancelled; and Venus and Mercury face each other across the D9 1st–7th axis, each in the other's sign of exaltation. This is an intimate, interdependent pattern: two people who complete each other's weak points, with a strong mental and conversational bond. The D9 7th lord Jupiter is in the D9 10th, an angle, which is a good sign for the partner's standing. Mars, the planet aspecting the D1 7th, is exalted in the D9.

Manglik status, with its references

Manglik (Kuja) dosha is checked from three reference points, using the houses 1, 2, 4, 7, 8 and 12.

Table 10. Manglik check

 | Reference | Mars counted from it | Manglik?

 | Lagna | 12th house | Yes

 | Moon | 9th house | No

 | Venus | 3rd house | No

So the chart is Manglik from the lagna only. Several cancellation rules in the literature apply. The most widely quoted is that for Cancer and Leo ascendants Mars is the yogakaraka and does not produce the dosha; one practitioner source goes so far as to call Leo lagna fully exempt. A Sanskrit rule quoted in several commentaries cancels the dosha when Mars is in Cancer or Leo. Some regional traditions also cancel a 12th-house dosha when Mars is debilitated there. The fair summary: a single-reference Manglik indication that is cancelled on multiple grounds. In traditional matching it would be handled by comparing with the partner's chart rather than treated as an obstacle.

For traditional compatibility matching (Ashtakoota), the relevant birth details are: Moon sign Scorpio, nakshatra Jyeshtha pada 1, Gana Rakshasa, Nadi Adi, Yoni Deer, Varna Brahmin, Vashya Keeta.

Timing

Saturn's own transit through Aquarius, her 7th house, from March 1993 to February 1996 (with a gap), followed by the start of the Venus mahadasha in June 1995 and the Venus–Venus sub-period to October 1998, is the classic period for marriage or a defining relationship in early adulthood. Saturn crossed the 7th again from January 2023 to March 2025, a time that would have put partnership responsibilities in focus.

In the coming years, relationship matters are likely to be especially prominent in:

August–October 2029 and May–September 2030, when Saturn in Taurus and Jupiter in Libra both aspect the 7th house. This combination of both slow planets influencing the same house is the "double transit" that K.N. Rao's school uses for timing partnership events. It falls in the Moon–Venus sub-period. For a married person, it typically shows as a period of renewed companionship, redefined roles, a joint project, or a partner's major life event.

Saturn's transit over the natal Venus–Saturn pair in Taurus (from August 2029 and mainly April 2030 – May 2032), which is 7th from the Moon. Phaladeepika (ch. 26, sl. 22) associates Saturn 7th from the Moon with strain on the spouse and travel; it is a period to share burdens consciously.

Friendships

The 11th house (friends, networks, elder siblings) is empty, ruled by Mercury in the 12th, and aspected by Jupiter. Friendships tend to be fewer and meaningful, often formed through learning, spiritual or charitable settings, or with people at a distance. Jupiter's aspect makes friends a source of good counsel. The Jyeshtha Moon often plays the elder or protector among friends, which brings loyalty but also a tendency to carry other people's problems.

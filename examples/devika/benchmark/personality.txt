2Personality and temperament

Leo rising: the one who holds the centre

Leo is a fixed, fiery sign ruled by the Sun, symbolised by the lion. Writers across the tradition associate it with dignity, generosity, leadership, honour and a need to be respected; as a fixed sign it gives loyalty and a certain stubbornness. The lagna is empty of planets, so its expression depends on its lord, the Sun, and on the planets aspecting it. Only one planet aspects the lagna, and it is the best possible one: Jupiter, by its ninth-house aspect from its own sign in the 5th. Jupiter's gaze on the lagna is one of the classic protective factors in a chart. It adds ethics, optimism and good judgement, and softens Leo's pride into magnanimity. People are likely to experience her as warm, principled and quietly authoritative.

The lagna nakshatra: Purva Phalguni, the nakshatra of Bhaga

The ascendant falls in Purva Phalguni (13°20'–26°40' Leo), ruled by Venus and presided over by Bhaga, the Vedic deity of good fortune, marital happiness and the enjoyment of what one has earned. Its symbol is the front legs of a bed or a hammock, a picture of rest after effort. The commonly cited traits are charm, sociability, creativity, a magnetic presence and a love of comfort and beauty. Because the lagna's nakshatra lord is Venus, and Venus is the strongest planet in the visible chart (own sign in the 10th), the aesthetic and relational side of the personality is unusually well supported: taste, grace, hospitality and an instinct for harmony are part of who she is, and they show most in her work. The lagna is in the 2nd pada, which falls in Virgo navamsa, ruled by Mercury. Sources describe this quarter as the hardworking, practical, detail-oriented face of Purva Phalguni. The Leo warmth is real, but it is joined to conscientiousness and a sense that things should be done properly.

The lagna lord: the Sun in the 12th, in Cancer

The Sun, lagna lord and natural significator of the self, father and authority, is at 5°55' Cancer in the 12th house, in the nakshatra Pushya, ruled by Saturn. Cancer is a friendly sign for the Sun, so it is not weak by sign, but the 12th is the house of withdrawal, expenditure, foreign lands, sleep, institutions and liberation.

The texts are frank about this placement, and it is worth seeing what they actually say:

Parashara (BPHS ch. 24, v. 12) says that when the lagna lord is in the 12th the person lacks bodily comfort, spends wastefully and has a short temper — and he adds that this applies when the lord has no benefic aspect or conjunction. Here the Sun is joined by Mars and Mercury and the 12th house receives only Saturn's and Rahu's aspects, so the condition is partly met; but the lagna itself receives Jupiter's aspect, which modern authors generally treat as a significant softener.

Phaladeepika (ch. 8, sl. 4) gives the Sun in the 12th estrangement from the father, weak eyesight and loss of wealth and children. Such single-placement verdicts are not meant to be read alone; Jupiter's strength in the 5th, the house of children, argues strongly against the last of them.

Modern practitioners describe a 12th-house lagna lord as a private, inward self that works for something larger than itself and may live far from the birthplace.

Weighing these: a self that is genuinely dignified but not exhibitionist. She is likely to be at her best when serving, organising or protecting from a position slightly behind the front line, and to need regular solitude and good sleep to stay well. The "lack of bodily comfort" line is best read as a life in which rest has to be deliberately protected. In the navamsa the Sun is in Leo, its own sign, so the inner self-respect is intact even when the outer life is modest about it. Pushya, the Sun's nakshatra, presided over by Brihaspati, is traditionally the most nourishing nakshatra: her authority tends to be nurturing and advisory.

The company the Sun keeps

Ketu is only 3°31' from the Sun. This "eclipse" combination (a modern grouping called Grahan yoga) is commonly read as periodic self-doubt, indifference to recognition, or a tendency to downplay one's own authority, together with a detached, spiritual streak. Mars, combust within 15°27' of the Sun, adds heat and drive to the self but has to work through the Sun's agenda. Mercury, 22°38' away and not combust, gives the self a thinking, communicative edge. All four share Cancer, the sign of the mother, nurture, home and feeling: the core of the personality is protective and caring, and carries more emotional sensitivity than the Leo exterior suggests.

The Moon's nakshatra and gana: Jyeshtha and Rakshasa

The Moon's nakshatra, Jyeshtha ("the eldest"), is ruled by Mercury, with Indra, king of the gods, as its deity. Sources describe Jyeshtha natives as natural seniors and protectors, people who take responsibility for a group early and who carry the burden of leadership; its shadow side is vigilance that turns into control, sharp speech under pressure, and loneliness at the top. The avakhada lists her gana as Rakshasa. In matching tradition this simply describes a strong-willed, independent, intense temperament, quick to defend itself, and not a judgement of character. The Saturday birth, with Saturn as the weekday lord and placed in the 10th, adds a sober, dutiful, long-view streak that becomes more pronounced with age.

Planetary "age" states

Parashari astrology also grades planets by their degree within a sign (the Baladi avasthas, "infant to dead" states). In odd signs the order runs infant, youth, adult, old, dead over each 6°; in even signs it is reversed. The classical rule of thumb gives full results in the adult state and progressively less in the others.

Table 5. Baladi avasthas

 | Planet | Sign (odd/even) | Degree | State | Reading

 | Sun | Cancer (even) | 6° (5°55') | Mrita (dead), at the edge of Vriddha | Self-expression quiet; results come through others or late

 | Moon | Scorpio (even) | 18° (17°33') | Yuva (adult) | Full capacity despite the sign weakness

 | Mars | Cancer (even) | 21° | Kumara (youth) | Half strength, growing with age

 | Mercury | Cancer (even) | 29° | Bala (infant) | Promise matures slowly

 | Jupiter | Sagittarius (odd) | 7° | Kumara (youth) | Half strength, growing with age

 | Venus | Taurus (even) | 28° | Bala (infant) | Strong dignity, delivers gradually

 | Saturn | Taurus (even) | 23° | Kumara (youth) | Half strength, growing with age

Only the Moon is in the "adult" band, which is a useful counterweight to its debilitation: the emotional nature is fully active, even if it is intense. The Sun sits only five arc-minutes from the next band, so its "dead" label should not be pressed. Avasthas are a secondary refinement and sources disagree on their percentages; their message here is consistent with the rest of the chart, however: most planets ripen with age rather than early.

Synthesis of temperament

Putting these together: a warm, dignified, principled woman with a strong sense of duty and good taste, respected at work, who carries a deeper, more emotional and more private inner world than she shows. She is protective by instinct (Jyeshtha, Cancer), tends to become the "eldest" in any group she joins, and gives a great deal to people and causes, sometimes at the cost of her own rest. Her pride is real but is usually expressed as standards rather than vanity. She needs solitude to recharge. Under Jupiter's aspect her best instincts are generous and wise; under stress the Jyeshtha side can show as guardedness and sharp words, which pass quickly.

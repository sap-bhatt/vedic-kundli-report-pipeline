## Chart verdicts (computed; identical in every section, use exactly these)
These settle points that several sections touch. They are reference facts, not content to restate: use a verdict only where it changes the reading of the area being written, in a clause, and then match it exactly. Never re-explain a verdict another section owns (see Ownership). "Natural" nature (benefic/malefic) decides the quality of a planet's aspect and company; its functional role for this lagna (houses ruled) decides which matters it carries. When the two differ, say both (for example: "a natural malefic, so its aspect is hard in manner, but as yogakaraka it carries the 4th and 5th").

## Library entries that apply
- `yoga/malavya` (high, accepted) - present: Malavya (planet: Venus; sign: Taurus; house: 10; from_moon_kendra: yes; combust: no): Mahapurusha yoga of Venus: Venus in its own sign (Taurus, Libra) or exaltation (Pisces) in a kendra. It marks comfort, wealth, vehicles, beauty, artistic taste and a happy married and family life.
  - Results: material comforts, vehicles and good food; attractive appearance and refined tastes; happiness from spouse and children; talent in arts, music or design
  - Strengthened by: Venus exalted in the 10th (Raman no. 20 example); present from both lagna and Moon (Frawley); Venus strong and well aspected (Raman no. 22 remarks; Frawley)
  - Weakened by: Venus weak by debilitation in navamsa, association or aspect: yoga only nominal (Raman no. 22 remarks); poor aspects can ruin the planet's significations (Frawley p. 180); weak luminaries deny kingship (Saravali 38.22)
  - Disagreements: Same reference-point split (lagna only vs lagna or Moon) and moolatrikona addition (Jataka Parijata) as the other Mahapurusha yogas. Classical texts describe it mainly through looks and rulership; Raman reads it as materialistic comfort as against Hamsa's idealism; Frawley allows devotional expression.
  - Phrasing: Present as comfort, taste, charm and a good domestic life, scaled by Venus's strength; do not imply indulgence or infidelity as fixed traits; omit lifespans.
- `yoga/raja_kendra_trikona` (high, accepted_caveats) - present: Raja (kendra-trikona lords) (pairs: (pair: Mars, Sun; how: conjunct; houses_owned: (Mars: 4, 9; Sun: 1))): A link (conjunction, exchange, mutual aspect, or one placed in the other's kind of house) between the lord of an angle (1, 4, 7, 10) and the lord of a trine (1, 5, 9) is the basic Parashari Raja Yoga for status, power and prosperity. Modern writers stress that some such link exists in many charts, so the result depends on the strength, closeness and freedom from affliction of the two planets.
  - Results: rise in status, authority or public standing in the periods of the linked planets; prosperity and comfort (texts say 'becomes a king or equal to one'; modern writers read this as high position rather than literal kingship); fame and honour
  - Strengthened by: The lords are strong by sign (exalted, own, moolatrikona) and by divisional dignity (Parijatamsa and higher: BPHS 41.29-34; PVR 11.7.2).; The link is close in degrees: Raman (HJH) says within 12 degrees; PVR says full results need roughly 5-6 degrees.; The link involves the 10th lord (strongest angle) or the 9th lord (strongest trine) (Laghu Parashari sl. 17; Raman HJH).
  - Weakened by: One of the lords also owns a dusthana or 3rd/11th: BPHS 34.15 says such a lord does not produce Raja Yoga by mere relation.; Both lords blemished by other lordships: no yoga (Laghu Parashari sl. 15 as explained by Verma).; The 9th/10th lords also owning the 8th/11th (Laghu Parashari sl. 22).
  - Disagreements: BPHS 34.11-12 counts placement alone (a trine lord in an angle or vice versa) as yoga. Laghu Parashari, Raman and PVR require an actual relation (conjunction, exchange or aspect) and grade the mutual-angle and mutual-trine kinds as weaker. On blemished lords, BPHS 34.15 says an angle or trine lord that also owns an evil house gives no yoga by mere relation. Laghu Parashari sl. 15 allows the yoga if only one of the pair is blemished.
  - Phrasing: Present it as potential for rising responsibility and recognition, not guaranteed power or wealth. Because many charts have some kendra-trikona link, mention it only when the planets are strong and close, and tie it to the dashas of those planets.
- `yoga/yogakaraka` (high, accepted_caveats) - present: Yogakaraka present (yogakaraka: Mars; yogakaraka_house: (Mars: 12)): A yogakaraka is a single planet that rules both an angle and a trine from the lagna. It is a Raja Yoga in itself: Venus for Capricorn and Aquarius, Saturn for Taurus and Libra, Mars for Cancer and Leo. Its strength and placement decide how much status and prosperity its periods bring.
  - Results: fame, honour, financial prosperity and success in the periods of the yogakaraka when it is strong (Raman HJH); the planet acts as the chart's most reliable functional benefic
  - Strengthened by: Strength by Shadbala, association, aspect and occupation (Raman HJH).; Relation to another trine lord (Laghu Parashari sl. 20).; Placement in an angle or trine (BPHS 34.13 counts this as 'specially' yogakaraka).
  - Weakened by: Debilitation, combustion and similar states leave a planet 'dispossessed of strength' and unable to confer auspiciousness (Verma, Laghu Parashari sl. 20 note).; Raman's negative units: joined or aspected by malefic lords, in debilitation or an enemy sign.; BPHS 34.19-22 (Aries): Jupiter's good is lost if he is 'at the disposal of a malefic'. This principle is applied there to trine lords generally.
  - Disagreements: Frawley lists Saturn as the single yogakaraka for Gemini, but for Gemini Saturn rules the 8th and 9th, not an angle. BPHS 34.25-26 names only Venus as auspicious for Gemini, and Raman's HJH list omits Gemini.
  - Phrasing: Describe the yogakaraka as the chart's most supportive planet, whose periods tend to bring growth and recognition in proportion to its strength. Do not promise wealth or power, and say plainly when the planet is weak or afflicted.
- `yoga/neecha_bhanga` (medium, accepted_caveats) - present: Neecha Bhanga (Moon) (debilitation_lord: Mars; exalt_planet: none; grade: 2; c1_sign_lord_kendra: no; c2_exalt_planet_kendra: no; c3_D_E_mutual_kendra: no; c4_exalted_in_D9: no; c5_with_or_aspected_by_D_or_E: no; c6_lord_of_exalt_sign_kendra: yes; P_in_kendra: yes): Neecha Bhanga means cancellation of debilitation. A debilitated planet is relieved, and in many texts turned into a Raja Yoga, by conditions such as the lord of its sign, or of its exaltation sign, being in a kendra from the lagna or Moon, or the planet being exalted in navamsa or retrograde. Texts and modern writers say a well-cancelled debilitation can give rise from humble beginnings and even outstanding results.
  - Results: the weakness of the debilitated planet is removed or reduced (Frawley; Raman HJH); status or kingship in texts that treat it as a Raja Yoga (Phaladeepika 7.26-30; Jataka Parijata 7.13)
  - Strengthened by: Several cancellation factors at once: Frawley says debility must usually be cancelled in at least two ways to be effectively countered.; The debilitated planet also in an auspicious house, not the 6th, 8th or 12th (Phaladeepika 7.3, 7.28).; Strong lagna lord (Santhanam, note in BPHS vol. 1 ch. 24 example).
  - Weakened by: Raman (300 no. 246): a debilitated retrograde Saturn in the 10th can, without other relief, badly harm the career.; Rath: a debilitated planet in an exalted navamsa that is also retrograde gives only short-lived elevation.
  - Disagreements: Sign lord vs exaltation lord: Phaladeepika 7.26/7.29 and JP 7.13 accept either in a kendra, while PD 7.27 and 7.30 grade both lords together higher. Meaning of 'thaduchchanatha': the lord of the exaltation sign or the planet exalted in the debilitation sign. Raman prefers the latter, applies both in HJH, and calls it controversial.
  - Phrasing: Describe it as a weakness that turns into strength through effort or circumstance, often after an early struggle. Do not promise a sudden rise, and do not label the planet simply 'debilitated' when a cancellation applies.

- `yoga/amala` (high, accepted) - present: Amala (planets: Venus): A natural benefic in the 10th house from the lagna or from the Moon (BPHS and Santhanam: only benefics, no malefic there). 'Amala' means spotless: it gives lasting fame, clean character and honour, with wealth according to Phaladeepika.
  - Results: good, lasting reputation; honoured by employers or authorities; charitable and helpful to others; prosperity gained by fair means
  - Strengthened by: the benefic unaccompanied and unaspected by malefics (Santhanam's notes and example); strength of the occupying benefic and its aspects and associations (Raman no. 13); the yoga present from both lagna and Moon (Raman's example chart 17)

  - Disagreements: Exclusivity: BPHS (as read by Santhanam) and P.V.R. Narasimha Rao require only benefics in the 10th, so a malefic there voids it; Phaladeepika, Jataka Parijata and Raman just require a benefic present. Wealth: Phaladeepika and Jataka Parijata promise it; Santhanam stresses that BPHS does not. Bhava chart versus sign-based houses: Santhanam prefers the bhava chart.

- `yoga/sunapha` (high, accepted) - present: Sunapha (planets: Jupiter): Sunapha forms when one or more planets other than the Sun occupy the 2nd sign from the Moon. The texts tie it mainly to self-earned wealth, intelligence and standing, coloured by whichever planet forms it.
  - Results: wealth and property gained by one's own effort (BPHS 37.7-10; Phaladeepika 6.6; Raman, Three Hundred Important Combinations no. 2); intelligence, good reputation and a status 'equal to a king' in the classical idiom, meaning standing among one's own people (BPHS 37.7-10; Brihat Jataka 13.5); learning in the sastras and a peaceable, virtuous disposition (Saravali 13.4)
  - Strengthened by: benefic, well-placed or yogakaraka planets in the 2nd from the Moon (Raman no. 2: Venus as yogakaraka fortified finances in his chart 5); several benefics in the 2nd from the Moon, e.g. Mercury, Jupiter and Venus together (Raman no. 2); the 2nd from the Moon also falling in the 10th/11th from the lagna, which links the yoga to career and gains (Raman no. 2, Leo lagna illustration)
  - Weakened by: afflicted forming planets: Raman's chart 4 has five planets in the 2nd from the Moon with a debilitated Sun, Ketu and aspects of Saturn and Rahu, and he calls the yoga practically defunct (Three Hundred Important Combinations no. 2); the Moon joined with Rahu or Ketu, Rahu in the 10th from the Moon, or a forming planet debilitated or combust: the results become mixed (Jataka Parijata 7.107)
  - Disagreements: Varahamihira (Brihat Jataka 13.3, Sastri's notes) reports two minority methods that he rejects: Srutakirti counted planets in the 4th from the Moon for Sunapha, and Devasarma counted from the sign of the Moon's navamsa. Sastri adds a footnote that Parasara recognised these two views. Saravali 13.3 counts 30 varieties of Sunapha, while Brihat Jataka 13.4 and Raman count 31.
  - Phrasing: Present this as a supportive yoga for earning by one's own effort. Name the planet forming it and its condition, since that changes the flavour a lot. Avoid promising wealth or rank; say it supports them when the forming planets are strong.
- `yoga/dhana` (medium, accepted_caveats) - present: Dhana (1/2/5/9/11 lords) (pairs: (pair: Mars, Mercury; how: conjunct) | (pair: Mars, Sun; how: conjunct) | (pair: Mercury, Sun; how: conjunct)): Dhana yogas are links among the wealth houses: the 2nd (wealth), 11th (gains), 5th and 9th (fortune) and the lagna. The classic form joins the 2nd and 11th lords with the 5th and 9th lords. These yogas promise wealth in proportion to the strength of the planets involved.
  - Results: earning and accumulating wealth (Bhavartha Ratnakara ch. 2); financial prosperity in the periods of the linked lords (Raman HJH)
  - Strengthened by: The 2nd and 11th lords combined with the 5th and 9th lords rather than with other lords (Bhavartha Ratnakara ch. 2 st. 3-4).; Lords in own, friendly or exaltation signs (Uttara Kalamrita 4.5).; Strength and favourable disposition of the participating planets (BPHS 41.17; PVR 11.9).
  - Weakened by: 2nd and 11th lords combined with the 12th lord: much wealth is not acquired (Bhavartha Ratnakara ch. 2 st. 5).; 2nd lord with the 12th lord gives only ordinary prosperity in the 2nd lord's dasa (Raman HJH 2nd house).; Lords of the lagna, 4th and 9th in the 8th, or the 5th lord in the 8th, give financial losses in their periods (Raman HJH 2nd house).
  - Disagreements: The calculator's '1/2/5/9/11 lords' rule matches no single classical verse exactly. Bhavartha Ratnakara st. 3 centres the 2nd and 11th lords with the 5th and 9th. BPHS 41 centres the 5th and 9th lords and adds lagna-specific forms. Uttara Kalamrita 4.5 pairs the 2nd and 11th lords alone. Jataka Parijata 7.21 ties wealth to the 9th-10th lords plus the 2nd lord.
  - Phrasing: Speak of capacity to earn and build resources, which shows most in the periods of the lords involved. Never promise riches; Bhavartha Ratnakara itself says most charts get modest rather than great wealth.
- `yoga/parivartana` (medium, accepted_caveats) - present: Parivartana (exchanges: (planets: Moon, Mars; houses: 4, 12; type: Dainya)): Parivartana is a mutual exchange of signs: planet A sits in a sign of planet B while B sits in a sign of A. Phaladeepika counts 66 possible exchanges between house lords. It divides them into Maha (28, good houses), Khala (8, involving the 3rd lord) and Dainya (30, involving the 6th, 8th or 12th lords), whose results range from wealth and authority to misfortune.

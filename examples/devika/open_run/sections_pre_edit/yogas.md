## Yogas and doshas

**Malavya (Venus).** Venus sits in its own sign, Taurus, in the 10th house, and it is also in a kendra (an angle: houses 1, 4, 7 or 10) from the Moon, so the yoga holds from both reference points. Classical texts tie it to comfort, refined taste, vehicles and a warm home life. Venus is debilitated in the navamsa (the ninth-division chart), and some modern writers call the yoga largely nominal in that case. Expect it to show in taste, charm and domestic happiness more than as a guarantee of wealth.

**Raja yoga (Mars with the Sun).** Mars, lord of the 4th and 9th, is conjunct the Sun, the lagna lord, in the 12th house. A link between an angle lord and a trine lord is the classic route to standing and recognition. The two planets are about 15 degrees apart. Stricter modern writers want them within roughly 5 to 6 degrees for full results, and Raman allows 12, so this link is real but loose. Both planets sit in a dusthana (the 6th, 8th or 12th house), and Mars is combust, meaning so close to the Sun that its light is overpowered. Rising responsibility here tends to come through steady effort, most in the periods of Mars and the Sun.

**Yogakaraka (Mars).** For Leo lagna, Mars rules one angle and one trine, which makes it the chart's yogakaraka, the most supportive functional planet. Its position is mixed. Mars is debilitated in Cancer, but the debility is cancelled on three of six classical tests: Mars is exalted in the navamsa, and the lord of its exaltation sign sits in a kendra. That relief is substantial. Combustion and the 12th house keep it from being fully open, so its periods bring growth in proportion to its strength, not a sudden rise.

**Neecha bhanga (Moon).** The Moon is debilitated in Scorpio, and that debility is cancelled, though on only one of the six formal tests. Its weakness is therefore partly relieved, and classical texts say such a cancellation can turn an early struggle into later strength. The Moon's themes (mind, mother, emotional steadiness) are owned in the Mind section; here, the point is that the lesson is steadiness built over time.

**Budhaditya (Sun with Mercury).** Mercury is 22.6 degrees from the Sun and clear of combustion, so the yoga counts in its stronger form. It supports sharp intelligence and practical skill, and it is a common yoga, so its weight is medium. Mercury sits in the 12th and in an enemy sign, which keeps the gift modest.

**Amala (Venus in the 10th).** Venus is the only benefic in the 10th, which classical texts link to lasting reputation and clean conduct. Saturn shares the 10th with Venus, 4.8 degrees apart. The stricter reading says a malefic in the 10th voids Amala altogether. Phaladeepika and Raman require only that a benefic be present. Read it as a real but contested yoga: a reputation earned by fair means, with Saturn's company making public life more demanding.

**Sunapha (Jupiter).** Jupiter, a planet other than the Sun, sits in the 2nd sign from the Moon (Sagittarius), so Sunapha forms. Texts tie it to wealth earned by one's own effort, learning and a respected standing. Jupiter is in its moolatrikona (a strong sign position) and retrograde, which helps, though it also rules the 8th, so some gains come with testing. The Moon is not joined to a node, so the results are not mixed for that reason.

**Dhana (Mars, Sun and Mercury).** Three wealth-linked planets are conjunct in the 12th: Mars (4th and 9th lord), the Sun (1st lord) and Mercury (2nd and 11th lord). Dhana yogas promise earning capacity in proportion to the strength of the planets involved. Because they share a dusthana, earning here tends to take effort. Bhavartha Ratnakara itself says most charts reach modest rather than great wealth, so keep the expectation modest.

**Parivartana (Moon and Mars).** The Moon sits in Mars's sign Scorpio (4th house) and Mars sits in the Moon's sign Cancer (12th house): a mutual exchange of signs. Because the Moon rules the 12th, the classical reading calls it Dainya, linking home and comfort (4th) with rest, retreat and loss (12th). Phaladeepika reads such exchanges harshly; Frawley reads exchange as strengthening each planet's power. A balanced reading: two areas of life are closely tied, with effort and obstacles around 12th-house matters.

**Shakata (Moon 12th from Jupiter).** The Moon-Jupiter form of this yoga is present, but cancelled because the Moon is in a kendra (house 4). Classical texts describe fortune that rises and falls like a cart wheel; here the cancellation means that pattern is softened.

**Grahan (Sun with Ketu).** The Sun and Ketu share the 12th at 3.5 degrees apart. "Grahan" (eclipse) is a later popular name, not a classical yoga, and a same-sign conjunction is not an actual eclipse at birth. Read it as intensity around the Sun's matters (vitality, self-expression, father) that can turn inward or spiritual. Low weight.

### Doshas

**Manglik (Kuja) dosha.** Present from the lagna: Mars is in the 12th, one of the houses read for Manglik (1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 12). From the Moon and from Venus, Mars falls outside those houses, so those reference points do not apply. It is cancelled on three grounds: Mars is in a movable sign (Cancer), the lagna is Leo, and Mars is debilitated. Modern authorities from Raman onward say the dosha is often over-applied. In matching it is one factor among many, best framed as a Mars theme in partnership: energy, independence and friction to channel well.

**Kaal Sarp.** Not present, neither full nor partial. Venus and Saturn fall on the opposite side of the Rahu-Ketu axis from the other planets. It is a modern idea absent from the classical texts, and Raman and K.N. Rao give it little weight in an individual chart.

**Pitra dosha.** Flagged under the modern, broader definition: the Sun conjunct Ketu, the Sun with Mars, and the 9th lord (Mars) afflicted alongside the Sun and Ketu. The more specific classical combinations about progeny (centred on the Sun, the 5th house and the 9th and 10th lords) are not among the flags raised here. This is not a sign that ancestors cursed anyone. Ancestral remembrance rites are an optional, traditional practice; avoid any reading about having or not having children.

**Ganda moola.** Flagged: the Moon is in Jyeshtha, one of the junction nakshatras. The narrow classical stretch is tied to the Jyeshtha-Moola junction, with the most severe part at the end of Jyeshtha and the start of Moola. Whether the birth falls in that stretch depends on the Moon's exact degree (17 degrees 33 minutes Scorpio) and the birth time, so check that against the classical timing. The broader popular reading flags the whole nakshatra regardless. This is a birth-timing consideration, not a fact about the family or the child's future; classical texts prescribe purification rites, and shanti rites are an optional family tradition.

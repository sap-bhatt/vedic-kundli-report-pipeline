## Yogas and doshas

Your chart carries several planetary combinations. The most important ones involve Mars and Saturn, which sit together with the Moon in the 12th house, and Venus, which is the chart's most supportive planet.

### Yogas

**Raja yoga: angle and trine lords joined.** The classical Raja Yoga, a link for standing, authority and prosperity, forms when the lord of an angle (1st, 4th, 7th or 10th house) is linked with the lord of a trine (1st, 5th or 9th). Here it forms twice. Mars, lord of the 4th, sits with Saturn, your lagna lord, about 5.6 degrees apart. The Mars link is the stronger one: Mars is moolatrikona (in its strongest sign) in the navamsa, the divisional chart that shows finer strength. The Moon, lord of the 7th, sits with Saturn as a second link of the same kind. Two points pull the Mars link back. Mars also owns the 11th house, which the stricter texts treat as weakening such a link, and both planets sit in the 12th house, which the Spirituality section covers. Classical texts speak of kingship; modern writers read it as high position and recognition. Treat it as a tendency toward responsibility and recognition, most active in the periods of Mars and Saturn. The Mars period runs from 28 September 2025 to 27 September 2032. The Rahu sub-period running now ends in March 2027.

**Yogakaraka: Venus.** For Capricorn, Venus rules the 5th (trine) and the 10th (angle), which makes it the chart's yogakaraka, a single planet that serves both. It is exalted in Pisces and moolatrikona in the navamsa, which is real strength. Two things temper it. Venus sits in the 3rd house, neither an angle nor a trine, and it is joined with Rahu, while Mars aspects it. Raman, a modern astrologer, says a yogakaraka joined or aspected by malefic lords loses some of its support. So its periods are likely to bring growth and recognition in proportion to its strength, with some friction in the mix rather than an easy run.

**Chandra-Mangala: Moon with Mars.** The Moon and Mars share Sagittarius in the 12th house, about 10 degrees apart. This gives drive, earning energy and an enterprising, emotionally intense temperament. The classical texts (Varahamihira, Phaladeepika) give mostly difficult readings of this yoga. Raman, a modern astrologer, counts it as a good wealth yoga when the planets are strong and sit in the 2nd, 9th, 10th or 11th houses. Here it falls in the 12th, so read it as drive that needs balance rather than a promise of wealth.

**Sunapha: Mercury.** Mercury, a planet other than the Sun, sits in the 2nd house from the Moon (Capricorn), so Sunapha forms. The texts link it with wealth earned by one's own effort, intelligence and standing among one's own people. Mercury is a natural benefic, but it is retrograde (moving backwards in the sky) and combust, meaning close enough to the Sun (about 6 degrees) to be overpowered by it. The texts say a combust forming planet makes the results mixed. So this is a supportive yoga with mixed results: self-earned progress is the theme. Mercury's own character is covered under Mind.

**Dhana: Saturn with Mars.** Saturn, lord of the 2nd house (wealth), and Mars, lord of the 11th (gains), are joined. That pairing is the core of the classical wealth links, and Mars's strong navamsa position helps the earning side. It shows capacity to earn and build resources, most visibly in the periods of these two planets. Bhavartha Ratnakara, a classical text on this yoga, itself says most charts get modest rather than great wealth, so read it as steady earning, not riches.

**Parivartana: Mars and Jupiter exchange signs.** Mars sits in Sagittarius, Jupiter's sign (12th house), and Jupiter sits in Aries, Mars's sign (4th house). An exchange like this ties the two houses closely, each planet acting for the other. Because Jupiter is lord of the 12th, Phaladeepika classes it as the difficult kind, linked with effort, obstacles and tension around home and the 12th-house matters. Frawley, a modern writer, treats exchanges as generally strengthening both planets. Put it plainly: the result depends on the effort put in, and it points to working through obstacles. Jupiter's effect in the 4th is covered under Family.

### Doshas

**Manglik (Mars in a sensitive house).** Counted from the lagna and from the Moon, Mars sits in a Manglik house (12th from the lagna, 1st from the Moon). Counted from Venus it does not. The sensitive houses are 1, 2, 4, 7, 8 and 12. So the dosha is present from two of the three reference points. It is cancelled under the standard rule here because Jupiter aspects Mars. The classical texts describe friction in marriage from this placement; modern authorities from Raman onward say the label is often over-applied and treat it as one factor among many. A matching Manglik in a partner's chart is also widely taken to soften it. The marriage side is covered under Relationships.

**Kaal Sarp: not present.** Kaal Sarp would need all seven planets on one side of the Rahu-Ketu axis. Jupiter and Venus sit on the other side, so the pattern is absent in both full and partial forms. It is a 20th-century idea, not found in the major classical texts, and Raman gave it little weight in individual charts.

**Pitra dosha: raised in the broad modern sense only.** The Moon sits with Mars and with Saturn, which the broader modern definition counts. The classical form, from the BPHS, centres on the Sun, the 5th house and the 9th and 10th lords, and concerns having children. The Sun is not joined with Saturn, so that classical form is not raised here. Ancestral rites are a voluntary practice of remembrance, not a required fix. This is not a statement about family or children.

**Ghata (Mars with Saturn): a modern label.** Mars and Saturn sit together, about 5.6 degrees apart, in the 12th house. Practitioners call this "ghat dosha" and read it as forceful friction, but it has no classical standing as a natal dosha. The most useful reading is tension between drive and restraint, which can turn into steady perseverance. The same name is also used for an unrelated timing table, so only this natal meaning is in view.

**Ganda moola: present only under the broad reading.** The Moon is in Mula, one of the six nakshatras (lunar mansions) that popular practice counts as ganda moola, so the broad reading applies. The classical rule is much narrower: a few ghatikas (short time units) at the junction of Jyeshtha and Mula, linked in the texts with the wellbeing of the child or a parent at birth. The Moon is about 10 degrees into Mula, away from that junction. The Moon also moves slowly, so this is less sensitive to small birth-time errors than the lagna is. Purification rites (shanti) are an optional family custom, which the classical texts describe as removing the effect. Nothing in this chart calls for them.

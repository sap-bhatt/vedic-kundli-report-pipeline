- `yoga/raja_kendra_trikona` (high, accepted_caveats) - present: Raja (kendra-trikona lords) (pairs: (pair: Mars, Saturn; how: conjunct; houses_owned: (Mars: 4, 11; Saturn: 1, 2)) | (pair: Moon, Saturn; how: conjunct; houses_owned: (Moon: 7; Saturn: 1, 2))): A link (conjunction, exchange, mutual aspect, or one placed in the other's kind of house) between the lord of an angle (1, 4, 7, 10) and the lord of a trine (1, 5, 9) is the basic Parashari Raja Yoga for status, power and prosperity. Modern writers stress that some such link exists in many charts, so the result depends on the strength, closeness and freedom from affliction of the two planets.
  - Results: rise in status, authority or public standing in the periods of the linked planets; prosperity and comfort (texts say 'becomes a king or equal to one'; modern writers read this as high position rather than literal kingship); fame and honour
  - Strengthened by: The lords are strong by sign (exalted, own, moolatrikona) and by divisional dignity (Parijatamsa and higher: BPHS 41.29-34; PVR 11.7.2).; The link is close in degrees: Raman (HJH) says within 12 degrees; PVR says full results need roughly 5-6 degrees.; The link involves the 10th lord (strongest angle) or the 9th lord (strongest trine) (Laghu Parashari sl. 17; Raman HJH).
  - Weakened by: One of the lords also owns a dusthana or 3rd/11th: BPHS 34.15 says such a lord does not produce Raja Yoga by mere relation.; Both lords blemished by other lordships: no yoga (Laghu Parashari sl. 15 as explained by Verma).; The 9th/10th lords also owning the 8th/11th (Laghu Parashari sl. 22).
  - Disagreements: BPHS 34.11-12 counts placement alone (a trine lord in an angle or vice versa) as yoga. Laghu Parashari, Raman and PVR require an actual relation (conjunction, exchange or aspect) and grade the mutual-angle and mutual-trine kinds as weaker. On blemished lords, BPHS 34.15 says an angle or trine lord that also owns an evil house gives no yoga by mere relation. Laghu Parashari sl. 15 allows the yoga if only one of the pair is blemished.
  - Phrasing: Present it as potential for rising responsibility and recognition, not guaranteed power or wealth. Because many charts have some kendra-trikona link, mention it only when the planets are strong and close, and tie it to the dashas of those planets.
- `yoga/yogakaraka` (high, accepted_caveats) - present: Yogakaraka present (yogakaraka: Venus; yogakaraka_house: (Venus: 3)): A yogakaraka is a single planet that rules both an angle and a trine from the lagna. It is a Raja Yoga in itself: Venus for Capricorn and Aquarius, Saturn for Taurus and Libra, Mars for Cancer and Leo. Its strength and placement decide how much status and prosperity its periods bring.
  - Results: fame, honour, financial prosperity and success in the periods of the yogakaraka when it is strong (Raman HJH); the planet acts as the chart's most reliable functional benefic
  - Strengthened by: Strength by Shadbala, association, aspect and occupation (Raman HJH).; Relation to another trine lord (Laghu Parashari sl. 20).; Placement in an angle or trine (BPHS 34.13 counts this as 'specially' yogakaraka).
  - Weakened by: Debilitation, combustion and similar states leave a planet 'dispossessed of strength' and unable to confer auspiciousness (Verma, Laghu Parashari sl. 20 note).; Raman's negative units: joined or aspected by malefic lords, in debilitation or an enemy sign.; BPHS 34.19-22 (Aries): Jupiter's good is lost if he is 'at the disposal of a malefic'. This principle is applied there to trine lords generally.
  - Disagreements: Frawley lists Saturn as the single yogakaraka for Gemini, but for Gemini Saturn rules the 8th and 9th, not an angle. BPHS 34.25-26 names only Venus as auspicious for Gemini, and Raman's HJH list omits Gemini.
  - Phrasing: Describe the yogakaraka as the chart's most supportive planet, whose periods tend to bring growth and recognition in proportion to its strength. Do not promise wealth or power, and say plainly when the planet is weak or afflicted.

- `yoga/sunapha` (high, accepted) - present: Sunapha (planets: Mercury): Sunapha forms when one or more planets other than the Sun occupy the 2nd sign from the Moon. The texts tie it mainly to self-earned wealth, intelligence and standing, coloured by whichever planet forms it.
  - Results: wealth and property gained by one's own effort (BPHS 37.7-10; Phaladeepika 6.6; Raman, Three Hundred Important Combinations no. 2); intelligence, good reputation and a status 'equal to a king' in the classical idiom, meaning standing among one's own people (BPHS 37.7-10; Brihat Jataka 13.5); learning in the sastras and a peaceable, virtuous disposition (Saravali 13.4)
  - Strengthened by: benefic, well-placed or yogakaraka planets in the 2nd from the Moon (Raman no. 2: Venus as yogakaraka fortified finances in his chart 5); several benefics in the 2nd from the Moon, e.g. Mercury, Jupiter and Venus together (Raman no. 2); the 2nd from the Moon also falling in the 10th/11th from the lagna, which links the yoga to career and gains (Raman no. 2, Leo lagna illustration)
  - Weakened by: afflicted forming planets: Raman's chart 4 has five planets in the 2nd from the Moon with a debilitated Sun, Ketu and aspects of Saturn and Rahu, and he calls the yoga practically defunct (Three Hundred Important Combinations no. 2); the Moon joined with Rahu or Ketu, Rahu in the 10th from the Moon, or a forming planet debilitated or combust: the results become mixed (Jataka Parijata 7.107)
  - Disagreements: Varahamihira (Brihat Jataka 13.3, Sastri's notes) reports two minority methods that he rejects: Srutakirti counted planets in the 4th from the Moon for Sunapha, and Devasarma counted from the sign of the Moon's navamsa.   - Phrasing: Present this as a supportive yoga for earning by one's own effort. Name the planet forming it and its condition, since that changes the flavour a lot. Avoid promising wealth or rank; say it supports them when the forming planets are strong.
- `yoga/dhana` (medium, accepted_caveats) - present: Dhana (1/2/5/9/11 lords) (pairs: (pair: Mars, Saturn; how: conjunct)): Dhana yogas are links among the wealth houses: the 2nd (wealth), 11th (gains), 5th and 9th (fortune) and the lagna. - `yoga/parivartana` (medium, accepted_caveats) - present: Parivartana (exchanges: (planets: Mars, Jupiter; houses: 12, 4; type: Dainya)): Parivartana is a mutual exchange of signs: planet A sits in a sign of planet B while B sits in a sign of A. Phaladeepika counts 66 possible exchanges between house lords. It divides them into Maha (28, good houses), Khala (8, involving the 3rd lord) and Dainya (30, involving the 6th, 8th or 12th lords), whose results range from wealth and authority to misfortune.
  - Results: Maha: prosperity, authority, gifts from rulers, vehicles, wealth and children (Phaladeepika 6.34); Khala: fluctuating fortune, sometimes prosperous and sometimes in distress (Phaladeepika 6.33); Dainya: foolishness, enemies, harsh speech, obstacles in undertakings (Phaladeepika 6.33)
  - Strengthened by: Both planets strong; Raman (HJH) mentions Gopuramsa for the 4th-10th exchange.; Exchange between kendra and trikona lords counts as Raja Yoga (BPHS 34.11-12; 41.28; PVR 11.7.1).; Frawley (p. 181) says an exchange can integrate the planets' power more than each simply being in its own sign.
  - Weakened by: Involvement of the 6th, 8th or 12th lord (Dainya) or the 3rd lord (Khala) (Phaladeepika 6.32-33).; Exchange of the 2nd and 12th lords is a yoga for poverty (Frawley p. 181).
  - Disagreements: Phaladeepika labels every exchange touching the 6th, 8th or 12th lord as Dainya, with bad results. Rath reads a lagna-6th exchange as a Raja Yoga that removes the 6th-house harm (chart 14). Frawley treats exchange as generally strengthening. Raman's 4th-6th exchange gives lands from enemies rather than misery.
  - Phrasing: Explain an exchange as two areas of life becoming closely tied, each planet acting for the other. Say which kind (Maha, Khala or Dainya) it is from the houses involved. Avoid harsh language for Dainya; say it points to effort and obstacles around the dusthana matters.

  - Phrasing: Separate the classical idea (specific BPHS combinations about progeny) from the broad modern label. Do not tell anyone their ancestors cursed them or that misfortune is ancestral punishment. Ancestral rites can be mentioned as a traditional, voluntary practice of remembrance, not as a required fix. Avoid predictions about having or not having children.
- `dosha/ghata` (low, accepted_caveats) - calculator: present: yes; note: flag only; no reference points or cancellations computed: 'Ghata' or 'Ghatak' has two unrelated uses. The modern 'Ghat dosha' is Mars conjoined with Saturn, read as forceful malefic friction. The older 'ghataka' (destroyer) scheme is a muhurta/transit table of unfavourable sign, tithi, weekday, nakshatra and lagna counted from the natal Moon sign. Neither has a classical natal-dosha verse among the texts read.
  - Disagreements: The two meanings are unrelated: one is a natal conjunction (Mars-Saturn), the other a Moon-sign-based table of unfavourable times. Practitioner pages also differ on whether Mars-Saturn is merely hard or also productive (perseverance; ganeshaspeaks, via summary).
  - Phrasing: Do not present this as a classical dosha. For Mars-Saturn, describe tension between drive and restraint that can turn into perseverance. For the ghataka table, mention it only as traditional advice on timing, never as a danger to the person.
- `dosha/ganda_moola` (high, accepted_caveats) - calculator: present: yes; note: flag only; no reference points or cancellations computed: Ganda moola (gandanta) birth: the Moon at a water-fire junction (Revati-Ashwini, Ashlesha-Magha, Jyeshtha-Moola). Classically it is a narrow window of a few ghatikas, said to threaten the child or a parent, and BPHS gives purification rites. Popular practice extends it to the whole of the six nakshatras.
  - Results: classically: risk to the father (day birth), the mother (night birth) or the child (twilight birth) (Jataka Parijata 9.58)
  - Strengthened by: Abhukta Moola (end of Jyeshtha / start of Moola) is the most severe, because the deities Indra and Rakshasa are opposed (BPHS 93.1-2)
  - Weakened by: see `cancellations`
  - Disagreements: Width: 2+2 ghatikas per junction (BPHS 92.3) vs the last pada of the nakshatra (Rath, Crux p. 241) vs whole nakshatras (popular). Abhukta Moola: 6+8 ghatikas (BPHS 92.5) vs a single ghatika at the junction (Jataka Parijata 9.45); secondary sources also cite 5+8 and versions attributed to Narada (2+2) and Brihaspati (1) (search summary). Which relative is affected: by day/night/twilight (Jataka Parijata 9.58) vs by nakshatra (Drik Panchang).
  - Phrasing: Say it is a traditional birth-timing consideration and check whether the birth falls in the narrow classical window or only in the broad popular one. Never tell parents a child endangers them or the family. Shanti rites can be mentioned as an optional family tradition; stress that the classical texts themselves say the rites remove the effects.

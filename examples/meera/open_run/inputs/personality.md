  - Weakened by: Same nadi for both partners is a matching fault (nadi dosha).
  - Disagreements: Muhurta Chintamani records several views: regional exemptions for the side nadis (Adi, Antya), which partner each nadi harms, and four- and five-nadi schemes used in some regions instead of the three-nadi scheme.
  - Phrasing: Do not describe nadi as a personal trait. In matching, state nadi dosha calmly, mention the exemptions and remedies, and never repeat a prediction of death or childlessness.
- `karaka/atmakaraka` (high, accepted) - Mercury is the Atmakaraka: The Atmakaraka is the planet with the highest degree within its sign; it stands for the self or soul and is the 'king' of the chart, whose condition colours the whole horoscope and governs bondage and liberation.
  - For this section (personality): Murthy ch. 2: Sun as AK - courageous and enduring; Moon - bright and adaptable; Mars - bold, adventurous; Mercury - intelligent, drawn to material gain; Jupiter - self-confident, ambitious; Venus - gentle, fond of comforts; Saturn - principled, hard-working, prospers later. Rath: Sun must overcome ego; Moon be caring; Mars avoid violence; Mercury keep speech truthful; Jupiter respect teachers and care for children; Venus keep clean character; Saturn share others' sorrows; Rahu stay guileless though often deceived.
  - Strengthened by: exaltation, moolatrikona, own or friendly sign (Murthy ch. 2; B.S. Rao on 1.1.12); benefic conjunction or aspect (B.S. Rao on 1.1.12); conjunction with the Putrakaraka or Amatyakaraka for raja yoga (PVR 11.7.3, 11.8; Murthy ch. 7)
  - Weakened by: debilitation, combustion or an inimical sign (Murthy ch. 2) - though Rath reads combustion of the AK as giving spiritual insight; malefic conjunction or aspect (B.S. Rao on 1.1.12; Raman art. 129)
  - Disagreements: 7- vs 8-karaka: BPHS 32.1-2 records both views without choosing; Jaimini 1.1.19 says some authorities merge mother and son karakas.
  - Phrasing: Describe the Atmakaraka as the planet that shows the person's core drive and life lessons. Do not call a malefic or weak AK bad luck; frame it as the area of growth. Avoid predictions of imprisonment, death or moksha as certainties.

3Mind, emotions and inner life

The Moon in Sagittarius, in the 12th house

The Moon governs the mind, feelings and the way experience is digested. Here it is in Sagittarius, Jupiter's fiery, philosophical sign, in the 12th house of solitude, sleep, distance and release. It is a waning Moon on the eleventh day of the dark fortnight, about 51° behind the Sun, so its paksha bala (strength from lunar brightness) is low; many authors treat a Moon this far into the waning phase as weaker and more inward than a bright one.

Varahamihira's Brihat Jataka (ch. 17, v. 9) describes a person with the Moon in Sagittarius as having inherited means, a vigorous and poetic nature, skill in the arts, pride and a knowledge of dharma, someone who is won over by persuasion rather than force and who succeeds by her own efforts; the same verse adds friction with kinsfolk. Phaladeepika is severe about the Moon in the 12th (ch. 8, sl. 7), speaking of being disliked, of misery, humiliation and indolence. These lines are best read as describing the Moon's raw tendency in the 12th — a mind that can turn inward, feel unseen and lose energy — rather than as a verdict, and here the condition is substantially changed by Jupiter (below).

Sagittarius gives the mind breadth, idealism and a need for meaning. In the 12th, feelings are processed privately: she is likely to feel most herself in quiet places, travel, study, prayer, or caring work done out of sight.

Mula, the nakshatra of roots

The Moon's nakshatra, Mula (0°00'–13°20' Sagittarius), is ruled by Ketu and presided over by Nirriti, the goddess of dissolution, who clears away the old so that something truer can grow. Its symbol is a bunch of tied roots. Commonly cited traits are a research-minded, investigative temperament that wants to get to the bottom of things, a capacity for deep transformation, and a tendency to tear down and rebuild. The Moon is in the 4th pada of Mula, which falls in Cancer navamsa, the Moon's own sign; onlinejyotish associates this pada with emotional depth, a nurturing disposition and success in healing or spiritual fields. Mula is also one of the Ganda Moola nakshatras (see section 10); its 4th pada is generally regarded as the mildest of the four.

Saturn and Mars: a crowded heart

The Moon is not alone. Saturn is only about 4° away, and Mars about 9½°. Each adds something definite.

Saturn with the Moon is the classical signature of emotional seriousness: early maturity, duty, a preference for a few dependable relationships, and worry carried quietly. Modern authors describe the person as composed outside while working through a great deal inside, with the conjunction's strengths showing more clearly after the early thirties. Because Saturn is her own lagna lord, this is part of who she is rather than a burden imposed from outside.

Mars with the Moon forms Chandra-Mangala yoga (section 10). Emotionally it gives courage, quick reactions, protective instincts and a capacity to act decisively when something matters; its shadow is irritability, impatience and occasional emotional outbursts, especially when tired. Mars here is at the very start of Sagittarius, in the gandanta zone, which adds intensity.

The three together in the 12th describe a mind that works hard, feels deeply and carries a good deal in private. The most common lived pattern is long stretches of calm competence punctuated by periods of exhaustion or low mood, usually when she has given too much and rested too little.

Jupiter's aspect: the counterweight

Jupiter, the great natural benefic, aspects the Moon by its full ninth-house aspect from Aries, and it is vargottama. The Moon is therefore not left with Saturn and Mars alone: it is watched over by the planet of wisdom, faith and guidance, which is also the lord of the Moon's own sign. Jupiter in the 5th from the Moon (counted from the Moon, it sits in a trine) gives the mind a philosophical safety net: she can usually find a meaning or a teaching that helps her through difficult feelings. This is the main reason the harsher classical lines on the 12th-house Moon do not apply in full.

The Moon's surroundings

Mercury is in the 2nd from the Moon, which forms Sunapha yoga (a planet other than the Sun in the 2nd from the Moon). Phaladeepika (ch. 6, sl. 6) gives Sunapha self-acquired wealth, intelligence and renown, and Saravali's description of a Mercury-formed Sunapha, as quoted in secondary sources, emphasises cleverness, good speech and skill in the arts. In the emotional sphere this means that thinking, speaking and writing are her natural ways of processing feeling. Kemadruma (an isolated Moon) is absent.

In the navamsa, the Moon is in its own sign Cancer, the navamsa lagna lord in the navamsa lagna, with Rahu. Whatever the D1 Moon's difficulties, the mature inner mind is strong and caring; Rahu adds an intense, searching, sometimes anxious quality.

In plain terms

A deep, idealistic, caring heart, with a serious inner manager and a brave, sometimes impatient, protective streak. She feels more than she shows, processes it alone or far from the crowd, and is at her best when her feelings are connected to a larger purpose. The main emotional work of life is to rest before she is depleted, to let others help her as she helps them, and to trust the steady wisdom (Jupiter) that has always been there behind the weight (Saturn).

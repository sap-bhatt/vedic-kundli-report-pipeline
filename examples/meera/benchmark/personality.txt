2Personality and temperament

Capricorn rising: the patient climber

Capricorn is a movable earth sign ruled by Saturn, symbolised by the makara, a sea-creature with the front of a goat and the tail of a fish: a creature at home both on the mountain and in deep water. Classical and modern writers associate it with patience, practicality, responsibility, endurance and a long view of life; it is a sign that builds slowly and keeps what it builds. It is also movable (chara), so for all its caution it is an initiating sign that likes to set things in motion. The usual Capricorn traits are present here, but they are strongly modified by the planet sitting in the lagna, Mercury, and by the fact that the lagna lord Saturn is placed far from home, in the 12th.

The lagna nakshatra: Shravana, the listener

The ascendant falls in Shravana (10°00'–23°20' Capricorn), ruled by the Moon, with Vishnu as its deity and an ear (or three footprints) as its symbol. Sources consistently link Shravana with learning through listening, a receptive and attentive mind, a gift for retaining what others say, good speech and an affinity with teaching and the oral tradition; onlinejyotish's nakshatra lesson calls Shravana natives scholarly and articulate. The shadow side commonly mentioned is over-sensitivity to what others say, anxiety and indecision. Because Shravana is the Moon's nakshatra, the Moon becomes a co-ruler of the personality alongside Saturn, and the Moon's placement in the 12th (see section 3) therefore matters for how she presents herself, not only for how she feels. The lagna is in Shravana's 4th pada, which falls in Cancer navamsa, the Moon's own sign: onlinejyotish describes this pada as sensitive and nurturing, oriented toward public service. The practical Capricorn exterior has a soft, caring centre.

Mercury in the lagna: the articulate self

Mercury, the planet of intellect, speech, trade and adaptability, sits in the lagna at 24°38' Capricorn, in the nakshatra Dhanishta, ruled by Mars. Mercury is one of the two planets that gain dig bala (directional strength) in the lagna, so it is well placed to express itself through the person. Phaladeepika (ch. 8, sl. 11) gives Mercury in the lagna a person who speaks "sweetly and cleverly". Parashara's house-lord verses add two layers, because Mercury rules both the 6th and the 9th for this lagna:

BPHS ch. 24, v. 97: the 9th lord in the lagna makes a person very fortunate, respected by those in authority, of good character, attractive, learned and honoured.

BPHS ch. 24, v. 61: the 6th lord in the lagna gives a person who is famous, wealthy, proud, courageous and virtuous, but prone to ill-health and liable to be her own enemy.

Put together: a capable, well-spoken, intelligent person who wins regard through competence and knowledge, and whose main obstacle is often herself — over-work, self-criticism, or taking on others' problems as her own (the 6th-house side of Mercury).

Mercury is retrograde and combust, only about 6° from the Sun (classical orbs treat a retrograde Mercury as combust within about 12°). Traditions differ on how much combustion harms Mercury, which is never far from the Sun; the common reading is that the intellect is strong but works close to an authority — a parent, employer or institution — and may take time to find its own name. Retrogression adds motional strength (chesta bala) and a reconsidering style: she thinks things through twice and often reaches unusual conclusions privately before sharing them.

Ketu, from the 9th, aspects the lagna. This adds a quiet streak of detachment, a dislike of empty convention, and periods when she withdraws from the image others hold of her.

The lagna lord: Saturn in the 12th

Saturn, the lagna lord and also lord of the 2nd, is at 6°18' Sagittarius in Mula, in the 12th, with the Moon and Mars. Parashara (BPHS ch. 24, v. 12) says that when the lagna lord is in the 12th the person lacks physical comfort, spends heavily and has a short temper, but qualifies this: the result holds when the lord has no benefic aspect or association. Here Jupiter aspects Saturn by its full ninth-house aspect from Aries, so the harsher reading is only partly applicable.

A lagna lord in the 12th describes a self that is drawn away from the centre stage: toward distant places, institutions, spiritual or charitable work, behind-the-scenes roles, or a life lived partly away from her place of birth. Sagittarius, Jupiter's sign, gives this a principled and philosophical quality. In practical terms the self is generous with time and energy, sometimes to the point of depletion, and needs regular solitude and rest to stay well. The "short temper" line finds an echo in Mars sitting with Saturn: frustration is felt sharply when her effort goes unrecognised, even if it is rarely shown.

The Sun: in the 2nd, at the start of Aquarius

The Sun, significator of the self, the father and authority, is at 0°51' Aquarius, an enemy sign, in the 2nd house of family, speech and resources; it rules the 8th and is debilitated in the navamsa. This is a modest Sun: authority comes less from display than from carrying family duties and speaking plainly. Phaladeepika (ch. 8, sl. 2) is harsh on the Sun in the 2nd (lack of learning and wealth, stammering), but single-planet verdicts are not read in isolation, and Saturn's aspect on its own 2nd house protects it. The realistic expression is direct speech, strong family responsibility, and sensitivity about being seen and valued.

Planetary "age" states

The Baladi avasthas grade planets by degree within a sign: in odd signs the order over each 6° is infant, youth, adult, old, dead; in even signs it is reversed. Full results come in the adult (yuva) state and progressively less in the others.

 | Mercury | Capricorn (even) | 24°38' | Bala (infant) | Promise matures later

 | Jupiter | Aries (odd) | 1°47' | Bala (infant) | Delivers gradually

 | Saturn | Sagittarius (odd) | 6°19' | Kumara (youth) | Half strength, growing with age

No planet is in the adult band, and four are in the infant state. Avasthas are a secondary refinement, but the message is consistent with the rest of the chart: these are planets whose promise ripens with age and experience rather than arriving early.

Mars at 0°40' Sagittarius (and, by a wider definition, Jupiter at 1°47' Aries) sits at a water–fire junction called gandanta ("knot"), which adds intensity and a sense of transition to that planet's matters rather than any fixed result.

Synthesis of temperament

Putting these together: a composed, practical and articulate person, observant and quick to understand, with a strong sense of responsibility and a deep need for meaning. She presents a capable, well-organised face and is often the person others bring their problems to. Underneath is a sensitive, private and somewhat restless inner life that needs space, distance and quiet to recover. She is likely to be modest about her own authority, generous with effort, slow to ask for help and occasionally sharp when pushed too far. The birth on Saturn's day, with Saturn as lagna lord, adds seriousness and endurance; the strong Venus and Mercury add grace, skill and a voice that people listen to.

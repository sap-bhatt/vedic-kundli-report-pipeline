10Yogas and doshas

A yoga is a planetary combination with a named result; a dosha is a combination considered afflicting. Modern software detects dozens of them, often with loose definitions, so the useful question is not "is it present?" but "how strong is it, and does the rest of the chart support it?" The computation behind this report records which variant of each rule fired; that is reflected in the weights below.

Further strengths that are not formal yogas

Mercury in the lagna with directional strength: Jupiter and Mercury gain dig bala in the 1st house. The 9th lord placed and strong in the lagna is a classic indicator of personal fortune (BPHS ch. 24, v. 97).

Jupiter vargottama and aspecting the 12th-house group: the chart's main protective influence on the lagna lord, the Moon and Mars.

A strong navamsa core: the D9 lagna lord Moon in its own sign in the D9 lagna; Venus and Mars in their own signs; Jupiter vargottama.

Notable yogas that are absent

Malavya: Venus is exalted but in the 3rd, not a kendra, so this great Venus yoga narrowly misses. Its quality remains; its angular, public expression does not, which is why her success depends on initiative.

Gajakesari: Jupiter is 5th from the Moon, in a trine rather than a kendra. A Jupiter–Moon link exists by aspect, but not in the Gajakesari form.

Sasa and Ruchaka: the lagna lord Saturn and Mars are both strong planets for this lagna, but neither is in a kendra, so these Mahapurusha yogas are absent.

Budhaditya: the Sun and Mercury are in different signs.

Dharma-Karmadhipati (9th and 10th lords connected), Lakshmi, Saraswati and the Viparita Raja yogas are absent; fortune comes through the routes described above rather than through these classic combinations.

Kemadruma (an isolated Moon) is absent, which is good.

Synthesis

The dominant positive structures are the exalted yogakaraka Venus, the directionally strong 9th lord Mercury in the lagna, and the vargottama Jupiter whose aspect protects the 12th-house group. The 12th-house conjunction carries both a Raja Yoga and a Dhana Yoga, but sends their results inward and outward — to faraway places, institutions, service and spiritual life — rather than into conventional status. The afflicting factors (the Manglik indication, the Dainya exchange, the weight on the Moon) are real but each is matched by a mitigating factor. This is a chart in which success is made rather than given, and in which giving, distance and devotion are part of the success.

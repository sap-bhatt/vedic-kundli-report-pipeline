The 2nd house: the Sun, and its lord in the 12th

The 2nd house of accumulated wealth, family and speech holds the Sun, lord of the 8th, and its own lord Saturn is in the 12th. Parashara is cautious about both. The 2nd lord in the 12th (BPHS ch. 24, v. 24) gives courage but limited wealth, and a tendency to depend on others' fortunes; the 8th lord in the 2nd (v. 86) speaks of modest physical strength and of wealth that, once lost, is hard to recover. In plain terms, the 2nd house is not a natural reservoir in this chart: money comes in, but it is easily drawn away by obligations, expenses and the needs of others.

Two factors soften this. Saturn aspects its own 2nd house from the 12th, and a lord aspecting its own house protects it, rewarding a steady, documented, long-horizon approach. And Sunapha yoga, formed by Mercury in the 2nd from the Moon, is associated by Phaladeepika (ch. 6, sl. 6) with self-acquired wealth: what stays with her is what she has earned herself.

Dhana yoga in the 12th: Saturn and Mars

The lords of the 2nd (Saturn) and 11th (Mars) are conjoined, which the computation records as a Dhana yoga (wealth combination). Its location, the 12th, conditions it: the house of expenditure, but also of foreign lands, distant markets and institutions such as hospitals, universities and ashrams. Parashara's line for the 11th lord in the 12th (v. 132) is constant spending on virtuous causes. The combination produces money that moves: earnings from distant, institutional or behind-the-scenes sources, and spending on travel, relocation, property, learning and generosity. Wealth is built by directing these outflows into things of lasting value.

The Moon joins this pair, forming Chandra-Mangala yoga, which modern writers associate with earning through enterprise, bold action and property rather than inheritance. In the 12th it adds the same proviso: earning and spending are both energetic.

Property: the Mars–Jupiter exchange

The 4th lord Mars and the 12th lord Jupiter exchange signs. Parashara is not encouraging about either placement taken singly: the 4th lord in the 12th (v. 48) lacks home comforts, and the 12th lord in the 4th (v. 136) brings losses through property, vehicles and homes. But an exchange also places each planet, in effect, in its own sign, and Phaladeepika (ch. 8, sl. 14) gives Jupiter in the 4th a happy life with family, attendants and plenty. Onlinejyotish's lesson on Mars notes that a Capricorn rising with Mars as 4th lord can gain substantially from land and property. The balanced reading is that property is an important part of her finances, often bought, sold or held at a distance from where she was born, and that property decisions repay careful paperwork and patience rather than haste.

Gains vs. expenses

Gains (11th): the 11th lord is the current mahadasha lord, so income is an active theme of 2025–2032. Because that lord sits in the 12th, gains tend to come from distant, foreign, institutional or less visible sources, and tend to be reinvested quickly.

Expenses (12th): three planets in the 12th point to substantial expenditure, much of it chosen — on home, family, travel, health, learning or spiritual and charitable causes. Jupiter's aspect on the 12th keeps this spending largely purposeful.

Speculation: the 5th lord Venus is exalted, which supports good returns from creative or skilled work and sound investment judgement. Rahu with Venus, however, adds an appetite for quick or fashionable gains that the rest of the chart does not support.

Overall

The wealth picture is sound but not effortless. Earning capacity is good and grows with skill and reputation, especially through work tied to the exalted Venus (career, creativity, communication). The challenge is retention: the 2nd and 11th lords both sit in the 12th. The most reliable route to security is the Saturn route — budgets, savings, property held for the long term, and clear records — rather than the Rahu route of quick bets. On the date of this report transiting Rahu is in Aquarius, over her natal Sun in the 2nd house, which traditionally makes family finances, speech and unexpected outgoings prominent; it is a time for caution with loans and impulse spending.

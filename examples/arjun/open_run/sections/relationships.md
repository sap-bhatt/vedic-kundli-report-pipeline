## Relationships, marriage and partner

### The seventh house and its lord

The seventh house is Libra, the house of partnership and marriage, and its lord Venus sits in the fourth house, in Cancer. A lord placed in a kendra (a pillar house: 1, 4, 7 or 10) is a good sign for marriage, and this is the main support in this area. Raman, Braha and Rath read a happy marriage, comforts and a supportive home; the classical text BPHS describes a truthful, intelligent and religious person with an independent-minded partner. Venus is a natural benefic in this chart, so its own nature is kind. Its position is less comfortable, though: it sits in an enemy's sign and is aspected by Mars and by Saturn. The texts link that kind of affliction to strain in domestic harmony. Read this as a theme of patience and steady effort in the relationship, not a fixed outcome.

No planet occupies the seventh house, and two planets aspect it. Jupiter's aspect comes from the third house as a fifth-house aspect, which the classical scheme counts at half strength, so it is real but moderate support. Modern and classical sources that read it describe a wise, ethical and supportive partner, shared values, and a marriage that can weather difficulties. Rahu's aspect on the seventh is lighter still, since the texts disagree on whether the nodes aspect at all, so give it little weight.

### The Darakaraka and the navamsa

Mars is the Darakaraka, the planet with the lowest degree in its sign, which the texts use to describe the partner. Mars sits in Sagittarius, a sign friendly to it, in the ninth house, and Jupiter's aspect supports it. The partner may bring drive, directness, a sense of purpose and a strong independent streak. Mars is a natural malefic, so its manner is forceful rather than soft: friction in partnership is something to channel, not a sign of trouble. Some schemes name a different planet as Darakaraka, and the classical texts record both views, so take this as one reading.

The navamsa (D9) is the divisional chart the classical texts assign to the spouse, and modern authors widen it to shared values and the inner self. Here the navamsa seventh house is Capricorn, ruled by Saturn, which sits in the first house of the navamsa in Cancer. No planets occupy the navamsa seventh. Saturn in the first house links the partner closely with the self. The classical Saravali describes a Saturn-ruled seventh as a more serious, mature and reserved partner, sometimes older, with harder qualities that tend to grow on a relationship over time. Venus, the lord of the main chart's seventh, sits in Scorpio in the fifth house of the navamsa, a trine, though in an ordinary sign. That supports the marriage promise without the extra strength of its own sign or exaltation. The navamsa refines the main chart rather than replacing it, so where the two point in different directions the result is mixed, not one side chosen.

### Manglik status

Mars is in the ninth house from the lagna, the eleventh from the Moon and the sixth from Venus. The Manglik houses are 1, 2, 4, 7, 8 and 12, and none of these three reference points puts Mars in one of them. Manglik is therefore absent: Mars is counted from none of the three reference points. Mars does carry softening factors, since it is the lagna lord and Jupiter aspects it, and these would cancel the dosha if it applied. Because no reference point counts it, there is nothing to cancel. Modern astrologers also regard the dosha as over-applied, and the classical lists differ on which houses count, so it is only one factor among many in matching.

### When this area is most active

Two stretches bring Jupiter onto the seventh house itself, which the texts treat as a timing marker for partnership: December 2028 to March 2029, and October 2029 to January 2030. Saturn aspects the house from Aries during both, and the texts link that aspect with slower, more serious going, so both stretches favour patience and careful choices. From June 2033 to April 2036 the Mercury-Venus sub-period is ruled by Venus, the seventh lord, which brings partnership themes to the front in a neutral tone. These are timing markers, not dates on which anything has to happen.

### Friendships (eleventh house)

The eleventh house is Aquarius, ruled by Saturn from the second house, and the Moon sits in it. Raman reads the Moon here as a sign of many friends. Jupiter in the third house is read the other way: Raman gives it few friends. The sources disagree, so the honest picture is a warm, sociable temperament whose close circle is more selective than wide. Mars in Sagittarius carries a warning about rivals in the classical Brihat Jataka and in Raman. Treat it as friction to manage, not a fixed outcome.

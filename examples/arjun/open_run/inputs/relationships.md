Classical texts give the rule and also cancel it when both partners have it; modern authorities from Raman onward say it is over-applied.
  - For this section (relationships): Core area. Classical texts read it as danger to the spouse's life (BPHS 80.47-48); modern authors read it as conflict, dominance or difficulty in the spouse's life, and judge it only alongside the 7th house, its lord and Venus (Frawley; Raman).
  - Strengthened by: no benefic conjunction or aspect on Mars (BPHS 80.47); counted from Venus it is strongest, from the Moon middling, from the lagna weakest (Raman, Muhurtha pp. 98-99); reinforced by other affliction of the 7th house, its lord or the marriage significator (Frawley p. 181)
  - Weakened by: see `cancellations`; the two most widely shared are a matching dosha in the partner's chart (BPHS 80.49; Raman; Frawley) and benefic, especially Jupiter's, aspect or conjunction (BPHS 80.47 by implication; onlinejyotish survey)
  - Disagreements: Houses: BPHS and Frawley count 1, 4, 7, 8, 12; the Kerala verse Raman quotes counts 2, 4, 7, 8, 12 (no 1st); modern practice usually counts both lists (1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 12). References: BPHS counts from the lagna only; Raman and modern practice also count from the Moon and Venus, but Raman makes Venus strongest while dekhopanchang makes the lagna mildest and all three together most severe.
  - Phrasing: Say which rule the chart meets (houses and reference point) and list any cancellations before anything else. Never say the spouse will die or that the marriage will fail. Frame it as a Mars theme in partnership (energy, independence, friction to channel) and say it is common and is only one factor among many in marriage matching. Avoid fear-driven advice to reject a match or pay for costly rituals.

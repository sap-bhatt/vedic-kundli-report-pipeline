## Yogas and doshas

Two yoga patterns in this chart are built on the lords of the lagna and the angles and trines, and they carry real weight. Two others are present but softened. Among the doshas, one does not apply, one applies only in its popular form, and two are read more lightly than their names suggest.

### Raja yoga: links between the angle and trine lords

Raja yoga is the classic link between a lord of an angle (houses 1, 4, 7 or 10) and a lord of a trine (houses 1, 5 or 9). This chart has two such links, and they differ in weight.

The Sun and Moon link is the cleaner one. The Sun, lord of the 5th, sits in Leo, its moolatrikona sign. The Moon, lord of the 4th, sits in Aquarius and is neutral there. They aspect each other from opposite houses. The Sun is a natural malefic in this chart and the Moon a natural benefic, and both still carry the link. The Moon is strong mainly in the navamsa, where it is exalted, so its side is moderate. Neither lord owns a difficult house, so nothing reduces the link.

The Jupiter and Mars link is more contested. Mars, the lagna lord, sits in a trine (the 9th), which supports the chart, but it also owns the 8th house. Jupiter, lord of the 9th, owns the 12th and sits in an enemy sign. Because each lord owns a house of difficulty, the texts disagree on whether this link forms at all. Read as potential, it points to rising responsibility through effort, mostly in the periods of Jupiter and Mars.

Read this as potential for rising responsibility and recognition, not guaranteed power or wealth. Many charts carry some such link, so it counts most where the planets are strong, which is the Sun-Moon pairing here.

### Viparita Raja (Harsha): resilience

Mercury, lord of the 6th, sits in the 6th house itself, in exalted Virgo. The strict form of this yoga, which Raman and P.V.R. Narasimha Rao require, is met. The traditions read it as resilience: winning against competition and opposition, often after a struggle. Raman warns that a trouble lord's harm is softened rather than removed, so expect progress that is earned rather than easy. Mercury's navamsa placement is weak, which keeps the yoga in its moderate form. Some texts also ask for malefic influence on the 6th house, and there is none here. The periods of Mercury are when this theme shows.

### Kemadruma: formed but cancelled

Kemadruma arises when no planet other than the Sun sits in the 2nd or 12th house from the Moon. Those houses from the Moon, Pisces and Capricorn, are empty, so the basic pattern forms. Most texts then cancel it when a planet sits in an angle counted from the lagna or from the Moon. Both apply here: Venus is in the 4th from the lagna, and Saturn is in the 7th from the Moon. Jupiter's aspect on the Moon softens it further.

Old texts link the pattern with hardship, dependence on others and having to work hard for results. In this chart it reads far more lightly: periods of feeling unsupported and needing to build one's own resources, paired with the chart's strengths. Those themes would be read in the periods of the Moon and Saturn, the two planets that form and cancel it. Does that match your experience?

### Dhana: capacity to earn

A Dhana link is present in the form that joins the lords of the 1st, 2nd, 5th, 9th and 11th houses. Here Jupiter (9th lord) and Mars (1st lord) aspect each other mutually. The classic core, the 2nd lord (Venus) and the 11th lord (Saturn), is not part of this link, and Jupiter sits in an enemy sign. The yoga therefore points to a capacity to earn and build resources, shown most in the periods of Jupiter and Mars. The classical text Bhavartha Ratnakara says most charts reach modest rather than great wealth, and that is the right expectation here.

### Manglik (Kuja) dosha: absent

Mars is the 9th house from the lagna, the 11th from the Moon and the 6th from Venus. The dosha counts Mars in houses 1, 2, 4, 7, 8 and 12, with the 2nd included in some traditions. From none of the three reference points does Mars fall in those houses, so the chart is not Manglik from the lagna, the Moon or Venus. Its meaning for marriage is covered under Relationships.

### Kaal Sarp: absent

Kaal Sarp requires all seven planets to sit on one side of the Rahu-Ketu axis. Here the Moon and Saturn sit outside it, so the pattern does not form. It is a modern idea absent from the classical texts, and Raman and K.N. Rao gave it little weight in individual charts. Nothing further follows from it.

### Pitra dosha: popular form only

Popular astrology software flags this dosha here through two points: Ketu sits with Mars in the 9th house, and Jupiter, lord of the 9th, sits with Rahu. The classical form, built on the Sun and the 5th house, does not form. The Sun sits in the 5th in its strong sign of Leo, with Ketu's aspect falling on it but no node or Saturn beside it.

Do not tell anyone that their ancestors cursed them. Ancestral rites can be mentioned as an optional family tradition of remembrance, not as a required remedy. Classical texts link the dosha with questions of children, and nothing in this chart is a prediction on that subject.

### Jupiter-Rahu combination

Jupiter and Rahu are conjoined in Gemini, about 7.7 degrees apart. Popular astrology treats this as a dosha that clouds Jupiter's wisdom and counsel, a combination the texts associate with poor judgement, ignored advice and being misled. The classical texts do not define it as a yoga; a Jupiter-Rahu link appears only in their progeny combinations. The modern writer Rath reads it as advice going unheeded and being deceived in the areas the afflicted house rules.

Read it as a reason to check advice carefully and avoid blind trust, not as a sign of bad character or misfortune. Rahu's sub-period, running to June 2027, is the stretch to watch for this, and a good moment to test the advice you have received against what has actually happened.

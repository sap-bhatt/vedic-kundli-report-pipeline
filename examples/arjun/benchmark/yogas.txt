10Yogas and doshas

A yoga is a planetary combination with a named result; a dosha is a combination considered afflicting. Modern software detects dozens of them, often with loose definitions, so the useful question is not "is it present?" but "how strong is it, and does the rest of the chart support it?" The computation behind this report records which variant of each rule fired; that is reflected in the weights below.

Notable yogas that are absent

Bhadra: Mercury is exalted in its own sign, but in the 6th house, not a kendra, so this great "Mercury" Mahapurusha yoga narrowly misses. The quality of Mercury remains; it works through service and competition rather than public prominence.

Ruchaka: the lagna lord Mars is in a trine (9th), not in a kendra or in its own or exaltation sign.

Gajakesari: Jupiter is 5th from the Moon, a trine rather than a kendra. Jupiter's aspect on the Moon still gives part of the same protection.

Budhaditya: the Sun and Mercury are in different signs (Leo and Virgo).

Dharma-Karmadhipati (9th and 10th lords connected): Jupiter and Saturn have no link here, and Parashara adds that for Aries their association alone does not create a yoga in any case. Lakshmi, Saraswati, Amala, Parivartana and Chandra-Mangala are also absent.

Shakata (Moon 6th, 8th or 12th from Jupiter) is absent: the Moon is 9th from Jupiter, a favourable relationship.

Synthesis

The dominant positive structures are the lagna lord–9th lord Raja and Dhana yoga, the full Moon and own-sign Sun in mutual aspect, the Harsha yoga of exalted Mercury, and the lords of career and gains placed in the house of wealth. The afflicting factors are few and of modest weight: Guru-Chandal in the 3rd, a cancelled Kemadruma, and the broad Pitra flags attached to the 9th house. This is a chart without major doshas, whose fortune rests on his own initiative and skill, and whose main test is to keep principle and ambition (Jupiter and Rahu) working together.

2Personality and temperament

Aries rising: the initiator

Aries is a movable, fiery sign ruled by Mars, symbolised by the ram. It is the first sign of the zodiac, and classical and modern writers alike associate it with initiative, courage, speed, directness and a preference for action over deliberation. Phaladeepika (ch. 9, sl. 1) describes the Aries-rising person in physical and behavioural terms as restless, always on the move and quick-tempered, with a fierce streak; such sign-by-sign lines describe a raw type that the rest of the chart then modifies. The lagna holds no planet, and its only aspect comes from Ketu in the 9th. That means its expression depends heavily on the condition of its lord Mars, and that Ketu adds a note of detachment, sudden insight and a certain unpredictability to how he presents himself. People tend to experience him as energetic and direct, but harder to read than a typical Aries.

The lagna nakshatra: Ashwini, the swift healer

The ascendant falls in Ashwini (0°00'–13°20' Aries), the first nakshatra, ruled by Ketu and presided over by the Ashwini Kumaras, the twin physicians of the gods. Its symbol is a horse's head. Sources consistently link Ashwini with speed, freshness, independence, helpfulness and a gift for starting things, and with healing and rescue in the widest sense (onlinejyotish's nakshatra lesson calls the 4th pada "emotionally sensitive, with satisfaction in healing and service"). Its shadow is impatience: frustration when things move slowly. The lagna is in the 4th pada, which falls in the Cancer navamsa ruled by the Moon; modern writers describe this pada as the softest of the four, blending Ashwini's quickness with care and protectiveness. Because Ashwini is Ketu's nakshatra, Ketu acts as a co-author of the personality: the lagna lord is conjoined with Ketu, Ketu aspects the lagna, and Ketu rules the lagna nakshatra. That triple link makes detachment, intuition and a search for meaning more central to his character than the sign alone would suggest.

The lagna lord: Mars in the 9th, with Ketu

Mars, the lagna lord, is at 3°36' Sagittarius, in Jupiter's sign (a friendly sign), in the nakshatra Mula, ruled by Ketu. The 9th is the house of fortune, father, teachers, higher learning, long journeys and dharma (one's sense of right action).

The texts pull in different directions here, and it is worth seeing both:

Parashara (BPHS ch. 24, v. 9) is warm: the lagna lord in the 9th makes a person fortunate, liked by people, devoted, skilful and eloquent.

Mars also rules the 8th for Aries, and Parashara's line for the 8th lord in the 9th (v. 93) speaks of someone who goes against dharma and does not believe; Phaladeepika (ch. 8, sl. 10) is severe about Mars in the 9th, with a line about the father and about harshness. Modern readers generally take these as describing someone who argues with inherited beliefs and authority figures rather than accepting them.

The planetary details favour the positive reading. Mars is in a friendly sign, in a trine, and exchanges a full aspect with Jupiter, the 9th lord and a natural benefic. This lagna lord–9th lord link is the chart's most important fortune combination. The realistic expression is a person whose sense of self is bound up with principles, learning and a personal philosophy, who is willing to fight for what he believes, and who will often have to discover his own dharma rather than inherit it.

Ketu, only about five degrees from Mars, colours this strongly. Mars with Ketu is classically sharp, sudden and intense: courage that acts without hesitation, interest in philosophy, martial arts or technical precision, and a tendency towards abrupt decisions. Modern summaries of the Mars–Ketu pair stress detachment, insight and occasional impulsiveness. In Sagittarius, the sign of dharma, the combination is more likely to serve a cause than to be merely combative.

The Sun: own sign in the 5th

The Sun, natural significator of the self, vitality and authority, is in Leo, its own sign and moolatrikona, in the 5th house of intelligence, creativity, children and merit from past lives. For Aries lagna the Sun rules a trine and is a complete benefic. Phaladeepika (ch. 8, sl. 3) is cautious about the Sun in the 5th, mentioning reduced happiness, wealth and children, but also intelligence and a liking for travel through wild places; for a Sun in its own sign, most modern authors read the placement as one of the strongest possible 5th-house indications (jagannathhora's lagna-wise notes describe it exactly that way). In practice it shows a confident, creative intelligence, natural authority when he is in his element, pride in his own ideas, and a real capacity for leadership in education, management or creative work.

Planetary "age" states

Parashari astrology also grades planets by their degree within a sign (the Baladi avasthas, "infant to dead" states). In odd signs the order runs infant, youth, adult, old, dead over each 6°; in even signs it is reversed. The classical rule of thumb gives full results in the adult (yuva) state and progressively less in the others.

Table 5. Baladi avasthas

 | Planet | Sign (odd/even) | Degree | State | Reading

 | Sun | Leo (odd) | 17° | Yuva (adult) | Full capacity

 | Moon | Aquarius (odd) | 25° | Mrita ("dead") | Outward results muted; offset by full brightness

 | Mars | Sagittarius (odd) | 3° | Bala (infant) | Lagna lord matures slowly; strength grows with age

 | Mercury | Virgo (even) | 9° | Vriddha (old) | Strong dignity, but results arrive with experience

 | Jupiter | Gemini (odd) | 16° | Yuva (adult) | Full capacity

 | Venus | Cancer (even) | 15° | Yuva (adult) | Full capacity

 | Saturn | Taurus (even) | 20° | Kumara (youth) | Half strength, growing with age

Three planets (the Sun, Jupiter and Venus) are in the adult band. Avasthas are a secondary refinement and sources disagree on their percentages (onlinejyotish's avastha lesson notes they are relative ratings, not probabilities). Here the Moon's "dead" state is heavily outweighed by its being full; the more useful message is that the lagna lord Mars is young by degree, so personal confidence and physical drive tend to grow markedly through the twenties and thirties.

Other markers of temperament

Born on a Monday, the Moon's day, with the Moon full: emotional presence and sensitivity are unusually visible parts of his character. The nitya yoga at birth is Dhriti ("steadiness, fortitude"), traditionally linked with perseverance. The Moon's nakshatra lord is Jupiter and the lagna nakshatra lord is Ketu, both of which lean towards ideals and meaning rather than material ambition alone.

Synthesis of temperament

Putting these together: an energetic, direct and independent person, quick to start things and impatient with delay, with a confident creative intelligence and a strong sense of personal dignity. He is principled more than conventional, likely to question teachers and received wisdom, and happiest when his work and his beliefs line up. The Ashwini–Ketu thread gives him a helper's or healer's instinct and flashes of intuition; the Mars–Ketu pair gives courage that can tip into abruptness. He tends to show warmth openly (full Moon, Leo Sun) but keep his deepest motives to himself (Ketu). The young Mars and the effortful Saturn mahadasha suggest a person who becomes noticeably steadier, more authoritative and more sure of his direction from his late twenties onwards.
